# FounderOps startup-building framework findings

## Design principles

1. **Validate before scaling.** Lean Startup frames the startup as a set of experiments and emphasizes the Build–Measure–Learn loop, actionable metrics, and validated learning. FounderOps should therefore turn each framework answer into a hypothesis, an experiment, a metric, and a learning record.

2. **Talk to users and launch early.** YC's essential advice emphasizes launching quickly, talking to users, building what people want, and doing things that do not scale before investing in scale. FounderOps should make customer conversations and early manual tests first-class tasks.

3. **Focus on a narrow initial customer.** YC recommends finding a small group of customers who love the product rather than a large group that is mildly interested. FounderOps should require an initial beachhead segment, ICP, and problem severity before allowing broad market expansion.

4. **Use a prescriptive roadmap.** MIT's Disciplined Entrepreneurship framework presents a systematic 24-step process for first-time and repeat entrepreneurs. FounderOps should expose a clear sequence of stages, while allowing founders to revisit earlier assumptions when evidence changes.

5. **Progress is evidence, not document completion.** A founder should not be marked complete merely for filling a canvas. Completion should be tied to evidence such as interviews, observed behavior, signed pilots, retention, revenue, or validated experiments.

## Proposed FounderOps stages

1. Founder context and goals
2. Industry, location, market, and regulatory context
3. Problem discovery and customer interviews
4. Beachhead market and ideal customer profile
5. Jobs-to-be-done and problem severity
6. Solution concept and value proposition
7. Competitive alternatives and differentiation
8. Business model and pricing hypotheses
9. MVP scope and manual/concierge test
10. Distribution and first-customer plan
11. Experiment design and weekly evidence review
12. Metrics, operating cadence, and next decision
13. Funding readiness and basic financial plan
14. Founder health, cofounder alignment, and risk review

## Product implications

- Every stage needs a guided set of questions with examples and an explanation of why the question matters.
- Inputs must support both typing and voice capture. Voice should transcribe into the same editable answer field rather than creating a separate data type.
- The system should generate a recommended next action from the founder's answers, not only a static checklist.
- The dashboard should show current stage, evidence collected, key assumptions, overdue actions, and the single most important focus for the week.
- Framework outputs should be editable artifacts: problem brief, ICP, value proposition, experiment cards, customer interview log, business model snapshot, MVP scope, metrics scoreboard, and decision log.
- Personalization should begin with industry, segment, audience, location, business model, founder goals, stage, and time available per week.
